# Editorial rules from the approved one-minute plans

- Use the exact supplied title. Invent an original, fictional North Indian folk-horror story. Open on a specific impossible event a viewer can see or hear while the camera is already on its source. Give enough context to understand the event, then reveal the cause and rule through a physical consequence.
- Write short spoken clauses in natural North Indian Hindi and Devanagari. Read the lines in order. Cut repeated description, background narration that delays the opening, and explanations the action already supplied. Leave brief space for decisive sound cues and the last reveal. Use one voice at a time per shot.
- A character speaks only when their request, warning, or choice should come from them. Keep that character present with their face or mouth readable. A narrator can quote an off-screen voice as part of the exact narration. In narration shots, visible mouths stay closed. Assign each exact line once in script order.
- Plan one primary location and a small cast, usually two people. Name and lock repeatable features, adult proportions, wardrobe and voice. Draw reusable empty plates of the same geometry, with deliberate wide, detail, and reverse views. Keep the semi-realistic flat 2D Indian style, clear outlines, story-specific lighting and weather. Keep the same clothes, doors, furniture, prop appearance and positions until a visible action changes them.
- Give each shot one primary visible beat. Start just before it, with a precise pose, screen positions, and the object already in the correct place. If the line says someone picks up a key, the key is still on the table at the opening. Show the pickup in motion. Describe where the key and hand end up. A completed event may be the opening only when a preceding shot already showed that event.
- Include the concrete action the Hindi line asserts. For an unseen voice, backstory, or reaction, show the thing that carries that information or the listener's physical response. Avoid chains of unrelated actions, internal cuts, montages, or surprise camera reframing. Use a locked view or one motivated move that keeps the important action visible.
- Review each cut against the previous shot's ending. Carry forward prop custody, mark count, open/closed positions, movement direction and light. With a motivated angle or time change, explicitly bridge those states in the opening composition. A supernatural state change still needs a visible cause, an on-screen reveal, or a deliberately described off-screen transition with a cue.
- While revising, read the exact speech against the opening, action, ending and sound for each shot. Check the named cast, visible people and appropriate plate. Catch the material change before saving. Repair only the affected shot, its assets if needed, and its neighbors when their continuity also changes. Review the repaired sequence again.
